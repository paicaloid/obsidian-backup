Lesson 0003 · DeepSORT

The Appearance Descriptor: From Crop to Vector

What r actually is, why it lives on a unit hypersphere, and why matching checks a whole gallery instead of just the latest snapshot.

The gap Lesson 0002 left open

The cascade trace assumed d(2)(i, j), the cosine-distance gate, just worked — that some number came back saying "close" or "far" between a track and a detection. This lesson is what's actually behind that number.

From pixels to a 128-dim vector

Every detection crop is run through a small convolutional network — 2 convolutional layers followed by 6 residual blocks — that outputs a single 128-dimensional vector. That's it: no boxes, no positions, purely "what does this crop look like."

This network is trained once, offline, on a large-scale person re-identification dataset (~1.1 million images across 1,261 identities) — never on your footage, and not retrained at deploy time. At inference it's just a frozen forward pass. This is exactly why MISSION.md puts custom re-id training out of scope for now: the off-the-shelf network is the whole point, not a placeholder for one you'd train yourself.

Why project onto a unit hypersphere

The network's raw output gets L2-normalized — divided by its own length — before anything else happens. Once every vector has length 1, comparing two of them by dot product is comparing them by cosine similarity (⟨ri, rj⟩ = cos θ when both vectors have unit length). Cosine distance is then just 1 − cos θ.

Why bother: without normalization, a brighter or larger crop could produce a longer raw vector, and Euclidean distance would confuse "different magnitude" with "different identity." Projecting onto the unit sphere throws magnitude away entirely — only direction (what the crop looks like) remains comparable.

Today's win

You can now explain what r actually is (a frozen CNN's 128-dim output, L2-normalized onto a unit sphere), why cosine distance is the natural metric once that normalization happens, and why DeepSORT compares a new detection against an entire 100-entry gallery per track rather than a single running average — pose and lighting shift the embedding enough that "closest single memory" beats "closest to the average" for catching a track through appearance changes.
